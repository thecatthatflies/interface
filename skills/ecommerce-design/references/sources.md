# Sources

Sources behind the [E], [S] and [P] tags in this skill. Checked November 2026. Baymard's detailed guidelines are paywalled; only free articles and benchmark summaries were read, some through search summaries, as marked.

## Baymard Institute

- *Cart Abandonment Rate* (statistics page): 70.22% average across 50 studies, 2006-2025; abandonment reasons (40% extra costs, 20% slow delivery, 19% distrust, 18% account required, 17% too long, 17% errors, 13% returns policy, 12% total not visible, 10% declined, 9% payment methods); checkout averages 23.48 form elements (14.88 fields) against an ideal of 12-14 (7-8). https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate
- *Checkout Optimization: Minimize Form Fields* (2024): 11.3 fields average, 8 needed; 5.1 steps; the five field savings with failure rates (89%, 75%, 35%, 24%, 84%). https://baymard.com/blog/checkout-flow-average-form-fields
- *The Current State of Checkout UX* (2024, updated 2026): 56% offer accounts too early; 72% accordion summaries; 48% speed not date; 83% static cut-off; 95% quantity selectors; 53% fulfilment options; 50% required and optional marking; 47% phone unexplained; 68% gifting in cart; related articles on guest checkout prominence (47%) and adaptive error messages (98%). https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-ux
- *Make "Guest Checkout" the Most Prominent Option*. https://baymard.com/blog/make-guest-checkout-prominent
- *Product Page UX* (2023, updated 2026): 57% size buttons; 37% in-scale image; 23% on-model; 89% save without account; 81% unit price; 67% total-cost estimate; 44% return policy; 78% gifting; 89% responding to negative reviews; 63% reviewer images. https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux
- Product list and filtering research (2015, 19-site think-aloud study and 50-site benchmark): 67-90% vs 17-33% abandonment; 38% list-item attributes not filterable; 32% no applied-filter overview; 61% key filters not promoted; 64% lack the four sort types. Read via Baymard's research pages and *The Current State of E-Commerce Filtering*, Smashing Magazine 2015.
- *Infinite Scrolling, Pagination or "Load More" Buttons?* (Holst, Smashing Magazine 2016). https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2016/03/pagination-infinite-scrolling-load-more-buttons/

## Standards, platform docs and law [S]

- Google Search Central, *Merchant listing (Product, Offer) structured data*: required and recommended properties, availability values, shipping and return policy, preference for markup in initial HTML. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/merchant-listing
- Google Search Central, product variant structured data (`ProductGroup`). https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product-variants
- schema.org `Product`, `ProductGroup`, `Offer`, `OfferShippingDetails`, `MerchantReturnPolicy`, `ItemAvailability`.
- WCAG 2.2: 1.2.2, 1.3.1, 1.3.5, 1.4.13, 2.1.1, 2.5.7, 4.1.2. ARIA APG combobox pattern.
- W3C, *Inaccessibility of CAPTCHA*. https://www.w3.org/TR/turingtest/
- Stripe, *Idempotent requests*. https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests
- FTC, 16 CFR Part 465 (consumer reviews and testimonials, effective 21 October 2024); 16 CFR Part 255 (Endorsement Guides); 16 CFR Part 464 (unfair or deceptive fees, live-event tickets and short-term lodging, effective 12 May 2025; read via law-firm summaries); Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act.
- EU Price Indication Directive 98/6/EC (unit prices; Art. 6a prior price for reductions, added by Directive 2019/2161).
- EU Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU, Art. 8(2) (order button wording) and Art. 22 (no preticked boxes for additional payments).
- European Accessibility Act, Directive (EU) 2019/882, applying to e-commerce services from 28 June 2025.
- Agentic Commerce Protocol (OpenAI and Stripe, September 2025) and Universal Commerce Protocol (Google and partners, January 2026, https://ucp.dev). Read through announcements and secondary coverage; specifications not reviewed in detail.

## Not verified here

- Baymard's 2025-2026 figure that 62% of sites do not make guest checkout most prominent appears only in secondary sources; this skill cites the 47% figure from Baymard's own article list.
- Conversion effects of specific filter layouts (horizontal toolbar vs sidebar) were seen only in summaries.
