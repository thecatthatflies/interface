# Strategy and vibe

Evidence tags: **[E]** empirical, **[S]** standard, **[P]** practitioner, **[J]** judgment.

## The idea comes from the subject

The strongest identities take one idea from the thing itself: its material, process, place, history, customer, or promise. Styles ("minimal", "bold", "premium") are not ideas; they are how an idea is executed [P: Wheeler, *Designing Brand Identity*; tasteskill's study of 247 gallery sites found the same split between memorable and forgettable work].

Ways to find it, fast:

1. List ten concrete nouns from the business (kiln, clay, glaze, 1,260°C, Leeds, batch, hands, fire, ash, shelf).
2. List what the customer gets that competitors do not.
3. Find a noun that carries that difference. Write the sentence: "X is like Y because Z."
4. Kill the first idea if any competitor could claim it.

## Personality

Pick three traits. For each, write what it means in practice and what it rules out. Aaker's brand personality dimensions (sincerity, excitement, competence, sophistication, ruggedness; Aaker 1997, *Journal of Marketing Research* [E]) are a useful vocabulary, not a template.

| Trait | Means | Rules out |
|---|---|---|
| Warm | Earth tones, plain words, visible hands | Chrome, slick gradients, jargon |
| Exact | Measured type, real numbers | Vague claims, decorative numbers |
| Unhurried | Space, few elements, slow motion | Urgency banners, countdowns |

## Naming the vibe

A vibe is useful when it is specific enough to decide something. "Clean and modern" decides nothing. "A pottery studio at 7 a.m.: raw clay, one warm light, nothing on the walls" decides palette, density, type, and photography. Write the vibe as a scene, then derive:

- palette temperature and saturation,
- type voice (bookish, mechanical, hand-made, institutional),
- density and whitespace,
- shape language (round, square, cut, organic),
- texture (flat, grain, paper, print),
- motion (none, slow, springy).

## Three directions

Each direction differs in idea, not just color:

```
Direction A: "Ember"
Idea: the flame inside the kiln door
Logo method: metaphor fusion (arch + flame), considered tier
Palette: ember orange, deep teal accent, warm grays
Type: Fraunces (display), Inter (text)
Feels like: a working studio, warm and exact
```

Choose with the brief: which direction is most distinct from competitors, works in the hardest context, and can be carried by a small set of rules?

## Brand voice

- Write three to five "say this, not that" pairs from real product moments (empty state, error, onboarding, receipt).
- Plain language wins for comprehension across audiences [S: GOV.UK content design; plainlanguage.gov].
- Voice stays constant; tone shifts with the situation (an error is calmer than a launch).
